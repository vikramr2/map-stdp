1. **Beats random:** yes, in every configuration except routing none. Spiking at $\lambda = 0.05$ is 6× random.
2. **Dual vs. exempt vs. none:**
   - dual and exempt are within one sd of each other (276 ± 26 vs. 258 ± 21);

4. **Race agrees with $T^K$:** yes. The KL is about twice the sampling floor (0.022 vs. 0.010), so the early-transient bias is real but small.

**What the structural term does.**

- **It helps.** It adds about 50 return in flow (258 vs. 210) and about 26 in spiking (140 vs. 114). It also reduces the spread across seeds, and it stops the $d_j$ ratchet: with $\lambda = 0$ there is no $\epsilon$ term at all, so $d_{max}$ climbs to 13 in flow and 19 in spiking.
- **The flow–spiking gap is large** (258 vs. 140). Candidate causes: the $d_j$ ratchet (still 2.7 in spiking with $\lambda = 0.05$), the noisy $\kappa$ in the eligibility, and the race transient.

## Reviews of the v1 results

**`snn-expert`** (spiking diagnostics in the scratchpad: 3–5 seeds × 100–300 episodes; preliminary):

1. **$d_j$ ratchet: rectified noise, and the floor value does not matter.** Lowering $w_{min}$ to $10^{-6}$ leaves the drift unchanged. The flow level ratchets too: $d_{max}$ 10–16 at $\lambda = 0$, because $\epsilon$ sits only inside $\lambda$, where $\lambda \epsilon = 0.05$ is about 100× too weak.
   - **Fix:** a separate term $-\epsilon_h (d_j - 1) \kappa_{ij}$, every frame, independent of $\lambda$ (derivation Eq. 4d). Spiking Monte Carlo: cosine 0.992 with its expectation, a column rescaling.
   - **Recommended:** $\epsilon_h = 5$ and spiking $\eta = 5$.
   - **Caveat:** homeostasis seemed to cost return at fixed $\eta$. Hypothesis: the ratchet acted as hidden learning-rate annealing, since the step in $T$ is about $\eta \Delta W / d$.
   - Low-passing $\kappa$ needs a second per-synapse trace, so it is not crossbar-native.
2. **Race transient.** Median first action spike at step 2–3. The deviation from Eq. 1d is 0.10–0.15 at steps 1–4 and reaches the noise level by step 6–8.
   - **Fix:** a burn-in of 10 steps. KL 0.011 against a floor of 0.010, from the start of training. Without burn-in it is 0.049 early.
   - Late-window spike-count shares match Eq. 1d to KL ≈ 0.0006.
   - **Return:** sharpened readouts win on return (argmax of counts 186, first-to-3 132, against 87 for burn-in 10), but they are not Eq. 1d. A matched score for $P_\beta \propto p^\beta$ is a $K$-vector (derivation Eq. 1e, Proposal).
3. **Sealing does not cost return.** $\lambda = 0.05$ beats $\lambda = 0$ in both backends. Sealing is the $D$ optimum under the clipped $M^{\ast}$, and it matters only when a task needs L→A paths.

**`neuroscientist`** (ranked):

1. **Sealing at persistence 0.996 is implausible as anatomy.** About 80% of inputs to a cortical area are intrinsic [Markov et al. 2011]. Long dwell times come from attractor dynamics on modest clustering, not from isolation. Sealed latent modules also become flow sinks.
   - **Suggestion:** a per-module exit floor as a dual, $g_{ba} = (M^{\ast}_a - \nu_a) \mathbb{I}[b \ne a]$, with $\nu_a$ by dual ascent toward $q_{min} p_a$ (Proposal; needs `/derivation-check`).
2. **Homeostasis.** It should act on all plasticity, as fast heterosynaptic plasticity does [Zenke & Gerstner 2017; Chistiakova et al. 2014], not only through $\lambda$. The evidence is postsynaptic [Royer & Paré 2003], so presynaptic output normalisation stays untested.
   - Against low-passing $\kappa$ across frames: it mixes credit across actions.
   - Soft bounds remove the rectification bias at its source [van Rossum et al. 2000].
3. **Race.** The one-spike race is an abstraction. The burn-in is plausible as tonic basal-ganglia inhibition or the subthalamic "hold your horses" signal [Frank 2006]. An N-spike race is more biological but needs a matched score.
4. **Task term.** Mostly supported. $\bar{h}_j$ is a presynaptic baseline, like $\bar{g}_j$. $\tau_e = 3$ frames is 0.3–0.75 s only under the 100–250 ms frame mapping.
5. **Encoding.** Conjunctive fields are an acceptable abstraction. Choosing $(\theta, \dot{\theta})$ is designer feature selection, and a full grid scales exponentially; random sparse conjunctive expansion is the scaling path.

**Merged decision (v2):**

- **Adopted:** separate $d_j$ homeostasis (Eq. 4d) with $\epsilon_h = 5$, at both levels; race burn-in $b = 10$; spiking $\eta = 5$. Both reviewers agree on all three.
- **Deferred:** the exit floor. Both agree sealing is the $D$ optimum. They disagree on whether to act now: the `neuroscientist` says yes, for plausibility, while the `snn-expert` finds no return cost on CartPole. It goes to M4, where latent paths matter.
- **Ablation:** sharpened policy (Eq. 1e), flow level only.

## Annealing test and model v3

**Hypothesis** (`snn-expert`): the v1 ratchet was hidden learning-rate annealing. The step in $T = W/d$ is about $\eta \Delta W / d_j$, and $d_j$ grew over training. **Test:** v2 with $\eta = 10$, either constant or annealed as $\eta / (1 + e / 300)$ over episodes $e$. Same protocol.

**Confirmed.** A constant rate stalls, and annealing more than recovers v1. In spiking at $\lambda = 0.05$, 4 of 5 seeds had an episode ≥ 475 in the last 100 under v3. Peak 100-episode means were 189–203, at or near the end of training on 3 of 5 seeds, so longer runs may help. The annealing is adopted as model v3: `--model v3`, now the default.

**v3 ablations (flow, $\lambda = 0.05$):**

- **none:** 39 ± 11, collapsed;
- **exempt:** 287 ± 21;
- **dual:** 327 ± 46.
  - Best single run: seed 4, 387 over the last 100.
  - $\mu$ still diverges (960–8900).
  - Its lead over exempt is about one sd (two-sample $t = 1.6$, $p = 0.15$, $n = 5$), and it was reversed in v2.

**v3 metrics:** $d_{max}$ ≤ 1.04, and race KL 0.010 (floor 0.010). L/A persistence is 0.996 in flow and 0.98 in spiking. $p_{\mathcal{A}}$ is 0.23 in flow and 0.39 in spiking.

## Outcome

**M2 criteria (SPEC §11), model v3:**

| Criterion | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Beats random (22) | Yes. Flow 287 ± 21 (13×); spiking 184 ± 13 (8×). |
| Dual vs. exemption vs. none | none collapses (39). Dual and exempt are within about one sd; the dual's $\mu$ does not converge. Exempt stays the default. |
| Modules persist | Yes, almost completely (0.98–0.996). The exit floor is deferred to M4. |
| Race agrees with $T^K$ | Yes: KL 0.010 against a sampling floor of 0.010, with the burn-in. |

**What M2 established:**

1. **The structural term is necessary for performance.** It more than doubles spiking return (184 vs. 77) and raises flow return by 100 (287 vs. 187), once routing is protected.
2. **Gap between flow and spiking** (287 vs. 184). Remaining suspects: the noise in the covariance $\kappa$ (SNR grows as $\sqrt{k}$), the 10-step burn-in shortening the useful window, and the spiking curve, which had not plateaued on 3 of 5 seeds.
3. **Dead ends:** the sharpened policy without an entropy floor, and the dual with a fixed $q^{\ast}$.

**Open, carried forward:**

- **Spiking:** longer runs, or $k > 100$, to see whether it closes the gap.
- **Dual:** learn $q^{\ast}$, or bound $\mu$.
- **Sharpening:** with an entropy floor or a $\beta$ schedule, and a matched spiking readout (first-to-$N$).
- **Exit floor:** M4.
- **Soft bounds:** for the rectified weak-synapse noise.
- **Environment:** `environment.yml` is still stale; this run used a venv with the package list (see Compute).

1. $\lambda = 0$ runs only with routing exempt.

## Problems for `snn-expert`, open

- **Task-term $d_j$ ratchet in spiking.** $d_{max}$ goes from 1.0 to 2.2 within 5 episodes at $\eta = 10$, but stays at 1.03 with $\eta = 0$. The noisy covariance $\kappa$ times $\delta h$, with $h \approx 7$, is rectified at $w_{min}$. The $\epsilon$ term sits only inside $\lambda$, where it is too weak. Options: apply $\epsilon (d_j - 1)$ to the task term as well, or low-pass $\kappa$ before it enters the eligibility.
- **The race reads the early transient.** The first action spike arrives at step 2–3, so it reflects direct C→A routing rather than the stationary $p_a$. The KL grows as the network modularises. A burn-in before the race may be needed.
- **$\lambda > 0$ seals the latent and action modules almost completely** (persistence 0.996), and $p_{\mathcal{A}}$ falls. Check whether this costs return.
