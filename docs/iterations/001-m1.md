**Setup:**

- **Network:** 80 excitatory neurons in modules C, L, L, A, A of 16 each. Within-module $p = 0.5$, controller recurrence $p_{cc} = 0.1$, C→L/A routing $p = 0.3$, other cross links $p = 0.05$, and $d_j = 1$ initially, for 1034 synapses. Defaults are $\alpha = 0.2$, $n = 20$, seed 0.
- **Spiking:** uniform threshold noise with $\theta = 0.5$, Bernoulli teleportation input, and a gain bisected until the external spike share $\hat{\alpha} \approx 0.2$. $\tau$ is defined as $1 + \theta / \text{leak}$ steps, so $\tau = 1$ in branching mode.

## Results

**1. Implicit walk.** The maximum L1 error over 32 stimuli is within the bound $2(1-\alpha)^n$ for every $n$:

| $n$ | Initial $W$ | Trained $W$ | Bound |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5 | 8.0e-3 | 5.4e-2 | 0.66 |
| 10 | 2.9e-4 | 1.4e-2 | 0.21 |
| 20 | 4.0e-7 | 1.3e-3 | 0.023 |
| 40 | 1e-12 | 1.1e-5 | 2.7e-4 |

Trained, modular networks mix more slowly, as the derivation predicts.

**2. Spiking error.** Module-level L1 error against the exact $\pi$, by frame window $k$:

| Mode | $k$ (window in $\tau/\alpha$) → L1 error |
| --- | --- |
| Branching (`leak=inf`) | 5 (1) → 0.77; 20 (4) → 0.33; 80 (16) → 0.15; 320 (64) → 0.070; 1280 (256) → 0.041 |
| Leak 0.25 ($\tau = 3$) | 20 (1.3) → 0.25; 80 (5.3) → 0.14; 1280 (85) → 0.091, a floor |
| Leak 0.125 ($\tau = 5$) | floor ≈ 0.15 |

- **Finite leak has an error floor** from spontaneous firing of integrated noise.
- **About 30 $\tau/\alpha$ is needed** to reach roughly 0.1 error, not the $4\tau/\alpha$ that derivation §2.3 estimates.

**3. Learning (3000 frames).** $D$ in bits, controller exit $q_{\mathcal{C}}$, and flow into the action modules $p_{\mathcal{A}}$:

| Rule | Routing | $D$ | $q_{\mathcal{C}}$ | $p_{\mathcal{A}}$ |

| Expected $\kappa$ | none | 5.88 → 4.04 | 0.24 → 0.134 | 0.35 → 0.26 |
| Expected $\kappa$ | exempt | 5.88 → 4.16 | 0.24 → 0.198 | 0.35 → 0.40 |
| Expected $\kappa$ | dual ($q^{\ast} = 0.75$ of initial) | 5.88 → 4.10 | 0.24 → 0.186 | 0.35 → 0.37 |
| Additive $G$ | none / exempt | 5.88 → 4.10 / 4.19 | | |

- **$D$ decreased under every rule.**
- **Routing starvation (§4.5) is confirmed only with a dense controller.** At $p_{cc} = 0.5$, $p_{\mathcal{A}}$ falls to 0.009 and $M^{\ast}_{\mathcal{C}}$ reaches 7.8. At $p_{cc} = 0.1$, about 20% of controller neurons have no within-controller target, which caps the starvation.
- **Exemption and the dual term** protected routing in every case. The dual term was infeasible at $q^{\ast}$ equal to the initial routing flow.

**4. Pairing counts against $J$** (branching mode):

| Count | Correlation with $J$ | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Causal | 0.79 | Acausal/causal = 0.73, so much of the count is chance. |
| Balanced (causal − acausal) | 0.48 | Correlation with $J - J^T$ is 0.96: it also subtracts real reverse transmission on reciprocal synapses (27% of synapses). |
| Covariance (causal − $c_i c_j (k-1)/k^2$) | **0.91** | Best. |

**5. Rates.**

- **Raw $W$:** spiking learning ran away, to rates of 0.65–0.71 per step, because $d_j$ drifted to about 11.
- **With presynaptic normalisation:** the mean rate stayed at about 0.055, the maximum at 0.24 or below, and no neuron was silent.
- **The expected rule** still pumps $d_j$ to 2–2.3. The $w_{min}$ floor turns noisy updates into a ratchet.

**6. Timing.** At N = 80 on the `jit` backend: 1.2 ms per frame at $k = 50$, and 4.2 ms at $k = 200$. About half of the time goes to SuperNeuroMAT's Python input-spike setup.

## Deviations from v0

- Presynaptic normalisation is applied in the spiking backend, as derivation §2.2 condition 1.
- The covariance count was added as a third option for $\kappa$.
- In spiking, the modulator table comes from the spike-estimated $\hat{\pi}$.
- Weights are bounded in $[10^{-3}, 1]$.

### `neuroscientist` review (plausibility)

1. **High: excitatory-only network with exact software normalisation.** E-only is implausible for any cortex-level claim. Exact division by $d_j$ is an abstraction; presynaptic resource limits are plausible but untested (Staras et al. 2010). Synaptic scaling is postsynaptic (Turrigiano et al. 1998) and would normalise rows, not $T$. **Before any plausibility claim:** per-module PV-like inhibitory pools with inhibitory STDP (Vogels et al. 2011).
2. **High: branching mode.** An acceptable abstraction (linear escape): one step ≈ 3–10 ms, valid at ≤0.05 spikes per neuron per step. The branching ratio 0.8 is far from cortex's ≈0.98 (Wilting & Priesemann 2018).
3. **Medium: covariance count.** The form is supported: Sejnowski 1977, Kempter et al. 1999, Gjorgjieva et al. 2011. It is more plausible than a fine-tuned balanced window. The frame-batched subtraction is non-causal; the biological variant uses online pre/post traces.
4. **Medium: frame duration.** Hundreds of steps is 0.5–3 s, plausible at the low end. State windows in spikes per module, since the error is sampling noise.
5. **Low–medium: controller.** Low recurrence is relay-like (thalamic), not cortical. In cortex, recurrence is about 2/3 of the excitation in the input layer (Lien & Scanziani 2013). The exemption is indexed by the presynaptic module, which is local only if controller inputs land on a distinct dendritic compartment.
6. **Low: weight bounds.** An acceptable abstraction.

**Disagreement, resolved.** The neuroscientist read the normalisation as patching estimator bias. `snn-expert` showed that the drift is clip rectification at $w_{min}$, not an expectation bias, since both terms conserve $d_j$ in expectation. Homeostasis without the output gain needs $\epsilon = 3$, and it is kept as the "presynaptic homeostasis" ablation.

### Open (carried to M2)

- **Inhibition.** With E/I pools, disynaptic inhibition gives competing modules negative covariance, which biases covariance $\kappa$.
- **Initial policy bias.** The return over the first 50 episodes is about 11, below random. Equalise the initial routing per controller neuron?
- **Race readout.** Measure its KL to Eq. 1d at $k = 100$.
- **Dual term.** Untested in the closed loop.
- **Online covariance.** The trace variant is untested.
